. The classification system I'd build for BetterTracker
Instead of only SUCCESS and FAILURE, use a more descriptive internal classification.
REJECTED
Negative

Explicit decision not to continue with the candidate.

OFFER
Positive

Explicit offer of employment.

INTERVIEW
Progression

Invited to attend or schedule an interview.

ASSESSMENT
Progression

Invited to complete a test, assessment or assignment.

PROGRESSED
Advanced to another stage, but the exact stage is unclear.

APPLICATION_RECEIVED
Receipt confirmed; no selection decision made.

How to measure whether your classifier is getting better
The research provides examples of phrasing, but not reliable frequency estimates of which phrases are most common across all employers. I wouldn't claim that a rule has 90% or 95% accuracy without testing it.
For your project, I'd create a manually labelled test set of around 100–200 emails or suitably anonymised examples, with a mixture of rejection, progression, offers, acknowledgement, unrelated messages and ambiguous cases.
Measure:
- Precision: Of the emails BetterTracker labelled REJECTED, how many were actually rejections?
- Recall: Of all actual rejection emails, how many did it detect?
- False positive rate: How often does it mistakenly identify a rejection or offer?
- Confusion matrix: Which categories get confused with one another?
For this particular app, I'd prioritise high precision for REJECTED and OFFER, even if it means flagging more messages for review. A missed automatic classification is inconvenient; incorrectly telling you that you've been rejected or offered a job is much worse.
Also test the application matcher separately from the email classifier. An email can be correctly classified as a rejection and still update the wrong application if a company has multiple open roles.

My recommendation for BetterTracker
Keep the following priorities:
1. Detect explicit rejection decisions.
2. Detect explicit offer decisions.
3. Detect interview and assessment invitations.
4. Distinguish progression from neutral acknowledgement.
5. Return ambiguous cases for review rather than guessing.

The biggest improvements over your original classifier would be switching to full email-body analysis, using explicit decision phrases instead of isolated keywords, and separating progression from final success. Those changes will be far more useful than simply adding 100 more keywords to your existing lists.

CLASSIFICATION RULES:

1. Search the full email body, not just the subject or Gmail snippet.

2. Match case-insensitively.

3. Prefer complete phrases over individual keywords.

4. Only classify emails that relate to an existing job application.

5. REJECTED:
   Require an explicit negative decision about the candidate.
   Do not reject based on "unfortunately" alone.

6. OFFER:
   Require an explicit employment offer.
   Do not classify "congratulations" or "offer" alone as OFFER.

7. INTERVIEW:
   Require an invitation, selection, or scheduling request.
   Do not classify a mention of a past interview as a new interview.

8. ONLINE_ASSESSMENT:
   Require an invitation or instruction to complete an assessment.
   Do not classify an assessment submission receipt as a new assessment.

9. PROGRESSED:
   Only use when the email clearly confirms progression,
   but does not specify an interview, assessment, or offer.

10. APPLICATION_RECEIVED:
    Detect acknowledgement emails without treating them as
    successful hiring outcomes.

11. PENDING:
    Preserve the existing application status.

12. ROLE_CLOSED:
    Do not automatically treat company cancellation as
    individual candidate rejection.

13. WITHDRAWN:
    Require evidence that the applicant requested withdrawal.

14. REVIEW_REQUIRED:
    Use when decisions are unclear, contradictory,
    conditional, corrected, or potentially sent in error.

15. OTHER:
    Ignore unrelated emails, promotions, and newsletters.

16. If both positive and negative signals appear,
    examine the final decision sentence.
    If still ambiguous, use REVIEW_REQUIRED.

17. Never automatically overwrite an existing OFFER
    or REJECTED status based on a lower-confidence email.

18. Save the matched phrase and email message ID
    for debugging and explaining the classification.

19. Ignore quoted email history when possible.

20. Prioritise accuracy over classifying every email.
